## One-Paragraph Abstract (rewritten)
The paper targets timing-driven detailed placement for FPGAs and argues that net-based timing optimization saturates before reaching the best critical-path improvement. It formulates critical-path improvement as a shortest-path optimization over candidate locations, allowing all nodes on a critical path to move together while controlling side effects on neighboring placement.

## Problem and Context
Standard net-based timing optimization does not directly optimize critical paths and can stall before achieving the best timing. FPGA detailed placement also has high-connectivity constraints that make some previous critical-path methods impractical.

## Method
- key idea: Model timing-driven detailed placement of a critical path as a shortest-path problem on a layered candidate-location network.
- assumptions: Assumes critical-path structure can be isolated and optimized with manageable side-path degradation through explicit delay limits.
- model/formula: The core formulation is a shortest-path optimization over candidate locations for all nodes on a critical path.

## Evidence

## Limits and Threats to Validity
The work is detailed-placement-centric rather than a full global-placement or accelerator-specific flow. It is older and may not reflect modern FPGA accelerator structure directly.
