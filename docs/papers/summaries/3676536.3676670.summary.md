## One-Paragraph Abstract (rewritten)
This paper argues that gate sizing should not remain isolated after placement, and instead fuses gate sizing with global placement using differentiable timing and leakage objectives. It is a recent example of how ICCAD papers pitch a coupled optimization flow and support it with strong timing and power metrics.

## Problem and Context
Separating design stages too rigidly can shrink optimization space and force later-stage fixes that damage earlier efforts. The paper targets that flow-level inefficiency.

## Method
- key idea: Jointly optimize gate positions and gate sizes during global placement through differentiable objectives and gradient-based optimization.
- assumptions: Assumes earlier-stage optimization freedom is valuable enough to justify coupling tasks that are traditionally separated.
- model/formula: Differentiable timing and leakage objectives drive the fused optimization.
